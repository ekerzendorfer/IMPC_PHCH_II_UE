1D Particle in a Box with Few Electrons: 1,3,5-Hexatriene ($6\text{ }\pi\text{-Electrons}$)

🔎 Skript-Nachschau: Open L22_The Shell.pdf, Slide 20 (1D Particle in a Box). Locate the energy formula $E_n$ for a box of length $L$.
📌 Extract from L22_The Shell.pdf (Slide 20 – Confined Electrons in a 1D Box):

A particle of mass $m_e$ confined to a 1D potential well of length $L$ ($V=0$ inside, $V=\infty$ outside) forms standing de Broglie waves with $L = n \cdot \frac{\lambda}{2}$ ($n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$). Its quantized energy levels are:

$$E_n = \frac{n^2 h^2}{8 m_e L^2}$$

By the Pauli exclusion principle, each spatial level $n$ accommodates at most 2 electrons ($\uparrow\downarrow$).

Problem: In conjugated polyenes, the delocalized $\pi$-electrons can be modeled as particles moving freely along a 1D box of length $L$. 1,3,5-Hexatriene ($\text{CH}_2\text{=CH–CH=CH–CH=CH}_2$) has $N = 6\text{ }\pi\text{-electrons}$ in a box of length $L = 0.730\text{ nm}$.
(a) Determine the quantum numbers $n_{\text{HOMO}}$ and $n_{\text{LUMO}}$ in the ground state.
(b) Calculate the minimum excitation energy $\Delta E = E_{\text{LUMO}} - E_{\text{HOMO}}$ (in $\text{J}$ and $\text{eV}$).
(c) Calculate the absorption wavelength $\lambda$ (in $\text{nm}$) and compare it with the experimental UV peak ($\lambda_{\text{exp}} = 258\text{ nm}$).

Step (a) – Orbital Filling ($6\text{ }\pi\text{-Electrons}$):

With 2 electrons ($\uparrow\downarrow$) per level, $N = 6$ electrons fill the lowest three levels $n = 1, 2, 3$:

$$n_{\text{HOMO}} = \frac{N}{2} = \mathbf{3}, \qquad n_{\text{LUMO}} = n_{\text{HOMO}} + 1 = \mathbf{4}$$
Step (b) – HOMO $\to$ LUMO Excitation Energy $\Delta E$:

Converting $L = 0.730\text{ nm} = 0.730 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m}$:

$$\Delta E = E_4 - E_3 = (4^2 - 3^2)\frac{h^2}{8 m_e L^2} = 7 \cdot \frac{(6.626 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J s})^2}{8 \cdot (9.109 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}) \cdot (0.730 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m})^2} = \mathbf{7.914 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J}} \quad (\mathbf{4.94\text{ eV}})$$
Step (c) – Absorption Wavelength $\lambda$:
$$\lambda = \frac{h \cdot c}{\Delta E} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J s} \cdot 2.9979 \times 10^8\text{ m s}^{-1}}{7.914 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J}} = 2.510 \times 10^{-7}\text{ m} = \mathbf{251.0\text{ nm}}$$

For a short chain ($6\text{ }\pi\text{-e}^-$), the 1D box prediction ($251\text{ nm}$) matches the experimental UV value ($258\text{ nm}$) remarkably well! Next, in Task R1, you will apply this exact 3-step recipe to $\beta$-carotene ($22\text{ }\pi\text{-e}^-$).

From 1D to a Square 2D Box ($E_{n_1,n_2}$) & De Broglie Acceleration

Problem: (a) When an electron is accelerated across an electric potential difference $U = 100\text{ V}$ (`L22_The Shell.pdf`, Slide 18 / Ex. 2), how does its de Broglie wavelength $\lambda$ depend on $U$, and what is $\lambda$ in $\text{pm}$?
(b) If electrons are confined in a square 2D plane ($L_x = L_y = L$, `L22_The Shell.pdf`, Slide 21 & `T02 Q4`), how is the energy $E_{n_1,n_2}$ expressed as a multiple of $E_0 = \dfrac{h^2}{8 m_e L^2}$?

Step (a) – De Broglie Wavelength & the Square-Root Exam Trap:

Kinetic energy $E_{\text{kin}} = e \cdot U = \dfrac{p^2}{2 m_e} \implies p = \sqrt{2 m_e e U}$. Inserting into $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{p}$ gives:

$$\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2 m_e e U}} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J s}}{\sqrt{2 \cdot 9.109 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg} \cdot 1.602 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C} \cdot 100\text{ V}}} = 1.226 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m} = \mathbf{122.6\text{ pm}}$$

⚠️ C/W Quiz Trap: $\lambda$ is inversely proportional to $\sqrt{U}$ (NOT inversely proportional to $U$!).

Step (b) – Energy Formula in a Square 2D Box ($L_x = L_y = L$):

In 2D, the energy is the sum of the independent $x$- and $y$-motions ($n_1, n_2 \in \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$):

$$E_{n_1,n_2} = \frac{h^2}{8 m_e}\left(\frac{n_1^2}{L_x^2} + \frac{n_2^2}{L_y^2}\right) \xrightarrow{L_x = L_y = L} \frac{h^2}{8 m_e L^2}(n_1^2 + n_2^2) = \mathbf{E_0 \cdot (n_1^2 + n_2^2)}$$

Task C1: Degeneracy & 6-Electron Ground State in a Square 2D Box (Try-Test T02, Question 4)

L22 Slides 21–22 • T02 Q4
🔎 Skript-Nachschau: Open L22_The Shell.pdf, Slides 21–22 (and T02_Try_Tests_Lecture2.pdf, Question 4). Let us model benzene simplified as a flat square molecule of side length $L$ containing $6$ confined $\pi$-electrons. Compare specifically the states $(n_1, n_2) = (1,1)$, $(1,2)$, and $(2,1)$.
(a) Express the energies of the three states $(1,1)$, $(1,2)$, and $(2,1)$ as multiples of $E_0 = \dfrac{h^2}{8 m_e L^2}$ (enter the integer factor in front of $E_0$):
$E_{1,1} =$ $\cdot E_0$
$E_{1,2} =$ $\cdot E_0$
$E_{2,1} =$ $\cdot E_0$
(b) Why do the two distinct quantum states $(n_1=1, n_2=2)$ and $(n_1=2, n_2=1)$ have the exact same energy (*degenerate*), and how are $6\text{ }\pi\text{-electrons}$ distributed across these states in the ground state?
✅ Complete Solution – Task C1 (T02 Question 4):
1. Energies: Using $E_{n_1,n_2} = E_0(n_1^2 + n_2^2)$: $E_{1,1} = (1^2+1^2)E_0 = \mathbf{2\,E_0}$; $E_{1,2} = (1^2+2^2)E_0 = \mathbf{5\,E_0}$; $E_{2,1} = (2^2+1^2)E_0 = \mathbf{5\,E_0}$ (and the next level is $E_{2,2} = 8\,E_0$).
2. Degeneracy: Because the box is square ($L_x = L_y = L$), a $90^\circ$ rotation simply swaps the $x$- and $y$-axes. Having one node along $x$ and two along $y$ ($(1,2)$) costs the exact same kinetic energy as two along $x$ and one along $y$ ($(2,1)$).
3. Ground-state occupation ($6\text{ e}^-$): Each spatial state $(n_1, n_2)$ holds 2 electrons ($\uparrow\downarrow$). Thus, the 6 electrons completely fill the lowest level $E_{1,1}$ ($2\text{ e}^-$) and the two degenerate levels $E_{1,2}$ ($2\text{ e}^-$) and $E_{2,1}$ ($2\text{ e}^-$).

Task R1: 1D Box Transfer – $\beta$-Carotene from Orbital Filling to Wavelength & Model Critique

Problem: The orange pigment $\beta$-carotene has a linear conjugated chain containing $N = 22\text{ }\pi\text{-electrons}$ along a box length of $L = 2.94\text{ nm}$ ($2.94 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m}$). Experimentally, $\beta$-carotene absorbs blue-green light at $\lambda_{\text{exp}} = 497\text{ nm}$.

💡 In-Class Discussion – Why does the 1D Box give $1239\text{ nm}$ (near-IR) instead of $497\text{ nm}$ (visible blue-green)?

What the model achieves: It correctly predicts that lengthening a conjugated $\pi$-system lowers $\Delta E$ and shifts absorption toward longer wavelengths.
What the model misses: The 1D box assumes a completely flat potential ($V = 0$) with equal $\text{C–C}$ bond lengths. In reality, long polyenes show bond-length alternation (shorter $\text{C=C}$ double bonds and longer $\text{C–C}$ single bonds) and electron–electron repulsion. This periodic potential opens an additional energy gap at the Fermi level (Peierls distortion), increasing $\Delta E$ and shifting $\lambda$ from $1239\text{ nm}$ down to $497\text{ nm}$.

✅ Complete Solution – Task R1 ($\beta$-Carotene, L22 Ex. 6):
(a) $22\text{ }\pi\text{-electrons}$ fill $n = 1 \dots 11$ with 2 electrons each $\implies n_{\text{HOMO}} = \mathbf{11}$, $n_{\text{LUMO}} = \mathbf{12}$.
(b) $\Delta E = (12^2 - 11^2)\dfrac{h^2}{8 m_e L^2} = 23 \cdot \dfrac{(6.626 \times 10^{-34})^2}{8 \cdot 9.109 \times 10^{-31} \cdot (2.94 \times 10^{-9})^2} = \mathbf{1.603 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J}} = \mathbf{1.001\text{ eV}}$.
(c) $\lambda = \dfrac{h c}{\Delta E} = \dfrac{6.626 \times 10^{-34} \cdot 2.9979 \times 10^8}{1.6032 \times 10^{-19}} = 1.239 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m} = \mathbf{1239\text{ nm}}$.
